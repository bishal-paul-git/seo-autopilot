# Traffic potential

## When to use
Ranking topics, gaps or pages against each other: which page to write or improve first, and whether a topic is worth it at all.

## The idea
A good page ranks for many related queries, so the worth of a topic is the traffic its whole cluster can bring, not the volume of its main keyword. The best evidence is how much the page that already ranks first gets, but only a paid data provider estimates that; the site's own data is always the first and most trusted source.

## Steps
1. **Own data first.** Sum the Search Console impressions of every query in the cluster over the last 28 or 90 days. Add clicks and the current average position. This is real demand the site already touches.
2. **Estimate the reachable share** from position: a page moving from position 8 to 3 can expect more clicks, but click-through by position varies widely by query and search features. Give a range, not one number, and say it is an estimate.
3. **Provider estimates, only if available:** if the owner has a provider key (for example DataForSEO, Ahrefs or Semrush), record the provider's volume or top-page traffic estimate with its name and date. Provider numbers are modelled estimates and often disagree; never mix them with Search Console numbers without saying which is which.
4. **No data:** write "no volume data" and judge on fit and business value instead. Zero impressions can mean no demand, or that the site simply does not appear yet.
5. **Business value:** a smaller topic that leads to enquiries or sales can beat a larger one that does not. Note the likely value (high, medium, low) and why, from the business's real offer.
6. **Confidence:** high (own data, many impressions), medium (own data, few impressions, or provider data), low (no data, judgement only).

## Never
- Invent search volumes, traffic or revenue figures, or present an estimate as a measured number.
- Promise traffic or rankings from a change.
- Round small numbers up, or extrapolate a 28-day sample to a year without saying so.
