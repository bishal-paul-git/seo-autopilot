# Answer-first copy

## When to use
Writing or fixing the opening of a page, or of a section that answers one question. Not for pages whose job is not to answer a question (a contact page, a product listing, a tool).

## Steps
1. **Name the question.** Use the page's main query, or the section's heading, phrased the way a person asks it. If you cannot state the question in one line, the page or section has no clear job: say so instead of writing.
2. **Answer it in the first paragraph.** About 25 to 60 words, a complete answer on its own: what it is, or the yes/no/it-depends with the deciding condition. No preamble ("In today's world…"), no restating the question, no teaser.
3. **Then the detail, in the order the reader needs it.** Steps as a numbered list, options as a short list or table, conditions and exceptions after the main answer, an honest "when this does not apply".
4. **Make it checkable.** Name things specifically: the product, the place, the rule, the date. Every number, price or claim must be in the site's verified facts or carry a source (the `facts` job checks this). If a figure is not available, write the answer without one.
5. **One definition, used everywhere.** If the page defines the business, a service or a term, use the same wording the rest of the site uses.
6. **Headings that match questions.** H2s say what each section answers. Readers scan them; snippets and AI answers often quote the paragraph under the matching heading.

## Checks before handing on
- The first paragraph answers the question with no other context.
- Nothing in it is unsourced, exaggerated or promised ("guaranteed", "best", "#1").
- It reads naturally aloud; it was written for a person, not stuffed with the query.
- The rest of the page delivers what the opening promises.

## Never
- Invent statistics, quotes or examples "to be quotable".
- Hide text, add text only for machines, or write different content for crawlers.
- Turn every paragraph into a question and answer; use it where a real question is answered.
